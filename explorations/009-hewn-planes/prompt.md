Use case: stylized-concept. A comparison sheet of THREE clearly separate sculptural wooden Y forks on the same warm neutral studio background. This is a material/shape range, NOT a motion sequence. Each object has exactly one lower trunk and two tapering upper arms, one junction, no extra branches. Same silhouette, scale, camera angle and neutral raking light for all three. LEFT: long hewn seven-sided wood, uninterrupted long planar cut facets with narrow secondary bevels and a small graceful longitudinal twist. CENTER: more emphatically carved five-sided wood, very broad flat asymmetric faces and two or three deliberate long shallow shaved planes, sharply legible edges at shallow angles. RIGHT: boldly hewn almost square/chamfered cross-sections, massive continuous flat faces and thin bevel strips, slightly organic taper and modest curvature, NOT perfect machined lumber. All three must look made from continuous real carved pale wood at the saddle junction, broad flat cuts throughout. Strong planar silhouettes and visible end grain. Minimal grain texture so it does not fake geometry. No bumps, no scalloped marks, no random low-poly triangles, no wrinkled or rounded tube surfaces, no repeated bulges along a branch. No moss, no bronze, no stone, no runes, no characters. Beautiful restrained painterly 3D product renders, high legibility of each plane. Landscape composition with generous separation.
