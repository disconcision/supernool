Clearer paths. A second contact. A body behind the pull.

This study keeps the original challenge and adds two ways to enact it. The mouse version drives a held pose directly. The body version lets Lehi’s walking load a damped spring while the hands stay on the tree.

Quieter guidance

Large target circles and text over the branches are gone. Possible moves use colored paths and small points. Spell names stay in the side panel, or in a separate band above the canopy when there is room. “Guide variations” also offers points only and branch emphasis. The hand stays near the tree while traveling between nodes; a 1.2-second return option is available for comparison.

Noolbox and the second hand

Open Noolbox to equip or unequip individual rules. This follows the 2D prototype’s loadout: the rules in your collection determine what can be enabled, and enabled rules determine the paths. All rules are supplied here; finding physical rule objects in the world is future work.

F or Shift-click pins a node with the other hand. Gestures that would move it, change its symbol or its incoming connection are excluded. To try a concrete example, pin the root, then grip its left branch: swap remains, while the competing regroup is excluded. Release the pin to restore the possibilities. A strict pin may prevent the whole solution until you release it.

The spring readout distinguishes body pull from tree progress. Appearance includes an inertia slider. The original six-step route was tested in both input styles. The two-button revision adds checked directional navigation and control handoffs.

What this does and does not settle

The spring supplies lag, resistance and settling along a valid rewrite path. It is not a rigid-body simulation of the tree. The strict pin filters incompatible gestures; it does not solve physical constraints. Obstacles can still limit where Lehi can stand, and fast motion can outrun mesh rebuilding.

Dynamic tree placement to separate gesture directions remains a promising later study. I would adjust the embedding as you approach, preserve the expression and anchored nodes, then freeze the targets during a grip. Silently reassociating the expression to improve layout would blur the distinction between positioning and rewriting.

Other retained threads: world pickups for rules, simultaneous multi-touch, a hinge-like second hand, protecting a subtree, and framing the scope of a rewrite with two contacts. These are recorded alongside the implemented changes in the project’s tactile-interaction roadmap.

Two-button update: E switches between walking and hand navigation. Arrows choose sigils in hand mode. Hold Space to anchor the hand, then use arrows to move Lehi and pull; release Space to settle. The second hand rests unless pinned. Held direction keys must be released and pressed again at a control handoff.
