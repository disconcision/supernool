# Idle hands playing catch · branch experiment

On `codex/idle-hand-catch`, the ordinary 018 route can play catch after roughly 7–11 idle seconds. The starting position is close enough to a small stone: open the scene and leave the traveller still. Under **Inspect → Appearance → Traveller → Idle hands**, choose **Catch only** to inspect catch independently, or **Finger walks only** to disable catch. The scenery-inspection idle has been replaced; see [IDLE-HANDS.md](IDLE-HANDS.md) for the shared scheduler, cooldown and hand turns. This is a reversible behavior experiment awaiting feedback.

One hand roves toward a real loose stone, turns palm down, wraps its fingers, and lifts it. The other perks up, opens its fingers and moves to a station forward of the traveller. The throwing hand aims its palm along the throw, swings back and down, accelerates through an underhand scoop, unwraps its fingers at release, and continues into a decelerating follow-through. A spinning ballistic stone travels to an offset receiving point. The receiving hand reacts and chases that point with bounded speed; a spatial proximity test determines whether it catches. A late reaction can miss. The stone bounces once and settles. The first miss ends the rally: both hands linger briefly, then ease back to escort.

The hands start 3–3.5 world units apart, forward of the traveller. Each successful catch invites both hands another 0.35–0.55 units outward. The existing obstacle footprints can limit either hand; each station stays within 4.6 units of the starting body position. Flight time grows with distance (roughly 0.85–1.5 seconds), while aim scatter and the chance of a late reaction increase across successive catches. Catching still uses actual spatial proximity. A safety timeout winds down an unusually successful rally after about 30 seconds; normal rallies end with a miss. Finished rallies impose a shared 12–22 second quiet period before any idle activity can start. These are modestly longer delays than the first prototype's 5–9 / 9–18 second ranges.

Walking and movement intent begin a disengagement animation. A held stone is lowered and unwrapped over 0.8 seconds; an airborne stone keeps its trajectory. Both hands then ease toward the moving traveller over 1.05 seconds. Stopping or turning during that interval does not restart the game. Tree contacts, pins, rewriting and explicit tree focus take priority immediately, including during a walking exit. Dropped stones remain where they settle for the rest of the page session; reload restores authored placements. The avatar-hit Easter egg is not implemented.

## Object boundary and implementation

`terrain.ts` explicitly registers six small, non-colliding loose stones (`100`, `102`, `103`, `110`, `111`, `115`). Larger stones, mushrooms, banks and the moving gates are excluded. A `CatchProp` carries an existing object, size, ground-support height and inspection contact. The approved fragment replacements remain children of those same objects, so the whole visible stone moves in both approved and original rock comparisons. No substitute prop, new asset or art generation is involved.

`idle-catch.ts` owns the sequence and stone motion; `lehi.ts` applies its palm poses and finger closure through the existing controller. Both imported articulated figures and the procedural checkpoint use this same behavior. `traveller.ts` continues to map finger closure onto the imported joints. Mouse/body manipulation, hand travel comparisons and algebra are unchanged. Inspection contacts track relocated stones.

This is procedural choreography with a fixed palm socket, not exact fingertip IK or rigid-body grasping. Start/receive positions use the existing obstacle footprints, but there is no full swept hand/stone collision solver. Ground support uses the loose stone's authored flat-clearing height. General terrain, arbitrary inventory objects and persistent object placement are outside this experiment.

## Verification

- `npm run check`, `npm run build` and `npm test`; the latter now includes `idle-catch-check.cjs`.
- Seeded simulations: 74 catches and 15 misses across 16 sessions; widening stations, first-miss endings, pickup attachment, position continuity, above-ground motion, bounded endings, walking exits and urgent interruption. Shared-controller checks distinguish walking set-down from contact, pin, rewrite and explicit tree-focus cancellation.
- `idle-catch-browser-check.cjs`: real Chrome on the running development server, both imported figures, pickup/notice/flight/catch, increasing separation, held and airborne walking exits, settling and the independent idle-activity controls.
- Existing hand anatomy/travel checks and the full six-rewrite mouse/body and body-only routes, with regression screenshots redirected to `.cache/idle-catch-regression/` to preserve the checkpoint captures. The body-only rerun passed against the built scene on a separate preview server after development-server reloads reset two earlier runs.
- Live-camera grip, lift/notice, flight, catch, widening and walking-exit screenshots are in `.cache/idle-catch-v2-review/`. Reviewed the finger/stone contact at intermediate poses and the arc in the ordinary full camera. One observed live rally reached 7.0 units of separation, caught six throws, missed the seventh, and disengaged.

The scene exposes `data-idle-catch` on `#world` for these checks (phase, stone, holding/falling state and session throw/catch/miss counts). It does not add gameplay HUD decoration.
