# Finger walks and shared idle scheduling

The scenery-inspection hover is replaced by a short ground excursion. The hands first drift away from the avatar. Once far enough away, a free hand descends to a clear patch near its current position, plants its fingers, walks a varied route, lifts off and returns. Each excursion chooses between a quick spider walk and a slower upright two-finger walk. In the spider walk, the four main fingers splay outward and stagger their steps so at least two support the hand while others recover. The thumb visibly flexes and sweeps in rhythm. Two-link finger solves use each imported finger's own joint lengths. The upright variant keeps its palm vertical, plants the two longest imported fingers, curls the spare fingers clear of the floor, and swings the thumb for balance. Its oversized strides alternate with hesitant forward progress and a side-to-side weight shift. The palm rocks and adjusts its height to the unequal reach of the two long fingers, keeping their middle joints nearly straight; swing clearance is a shallow 0.035 units. Finger solves account for splay and palm rotation. The upright gait now plants one of its two walking fingertips in world space for the whole loaded half-step. After the active figure’s fingers are articulated, a palm translation holds that actual fingertip fixed through the weight shift and route turn; the other finger swings, lands, and takes over support. Corrections carry across steps, so handover does not snap the palm back onto the nominal route. The active imported or procedural rig supplies its own contacts; hidden comparison hands cannot overwrite them. If the corrected path would enter scenery, the hand lifts from its last clear pose. The procedural checkpoint uses three fingers for the spider gait and two lengthened fingers for the upright comparison. No new art assets are added.

In the playable scene, routes start near the already drifting hand and stay 1.8–4.6 units from the body, typically walking about 3–5 units in total at 0.38–0.48 units/second. The upright variant takes shorter routes (about 1.9–2.6 units) at roughly one third of the spider walk’s speed, with a 0.54-unit gait cycle and shallow swinging steps. Initial travel headings mix in a modest 0.45-strength preference toward the current camera’s horizontal viewing direction. The preference follows camera orbit, preserves routes in every direction, and falls back to the original distribution for a directly overhead view. Each short path segment and its look-ahead are checked against the current bank/gate collision footprints, visible loose stones and fungi, with clearance for the hand. A blocked route stops the excursion and lifts the hand away. This is ground walking on the clearing's flat playable surface; climbing rocks, uneven-terrain contact, and full collision checking of the flying approach are not implemented.

A spider walk has a 24% chance of one stumble; an upright walk has a 48% chance. Either can stumble after at least 1.2 units of travel, provided the larger sideways fall sweep is clear. The upright hand captures the rendered position of a planted fingertip, tips around that fixed contact toward its supporting side, gives a small impact recoil, pauses, pushes upright around the same contact, then wobbles and shuffles its toes for 1.6–2.3 seconds before continuing. Route progress is frozen throughout the mishap. Movement interrupts any stage with a quick startle, then a lift that rights the hand and brings it home; tree interaction still takes priority immediately. The spider gait retains its existing sideways tumble. The shared quiet interval begins after the excursion ends, not during the daze. This is procedural comic timing, not rigid-body fall physics.

## Shared quiet time and hand choice

`idle-schedule.ts` arbitrates all idle activities. The first choice requires both the initial 7–11 stationary-second timer and sufficient distance from the avatar. After 2.2 stationary seconds, the hands start drifting with independent departure delays of up to 1.8 seconds. One chooses a modest cruising speed of 0.17–0.22 units/second and the other 0.27–0.34, with the quicker hand chosen afresh each time. Each eases in over 3.5–6.5 seconds, varies its pace on a separate rhythm, and follows broad curved bends with smoothed velocity. Each needs to reach its own randomly chosen 2.1–2.9 unit distance before it may initiate an activity. Targets favor the sides and rear of the avatar, with some front-facing excursions, and reject obstructed steps. Reaching activity distance now takes longer than the earlier straight drift; the timing depends on the wandering route and nearby obstacles. Finishing or interrupting either activity brings both hands home and starts one shared 12–22 second quiet period; catch cannot hand straight over to a walk after a voluntary miss. Only one activity runs at a time. Movement gives roaming hands and either activity a 0.2-second upward flinch and wrist jerk before the gentle lift-off/set-down and return. A held stone stays attached during the flinch; an airborne stone continues its trajectory and falls. Natural activity endings return without a startle. Tree contact or explicit interaction takes priority immediately, bypassing the flinch. Quiet drifting can resume during the cooldown, but another activity must satisfy both the timer and distance gate.

When both activities are available, the first choice is random and later successful activities alternate. Walks maintain an alternating hand turn. Catch prefers its alternating hand turn when both are eligible, but lets whichever eligible hand encounters a nearby stone initiate. In mixed mode, a pending catch gets 2.5 seconds with at least one hand beyond its distance threshold before a failed opportunity may fall back to walking. Checks still occur every 0.35 seconds; a nearby stone can start catch immediately during that window. The pending choice is retained while the hands depart and resets after movement, interaction, an activity start or a cooldown reset. This adds a short chance to encounter a stone without changing drift speeds, pickup radius, or shared cooldowns. There are no assigned personalities yet. The old behavior chose a nearby target and used the hand on that side, which could repeatedly select the same hand and relative spot. Catch requires a clear approach to a stone within 1.6 horizontal units of the initiating hand and chooses its nearest eligible stone. After the quiet interval expires, it checks every 0.35 seconds so brief encounters are not missed. It uses the hands' roaming bearings for stations 2.5–3.2 units out, then spreads them farther along their shared throwing line after successful catches. If both hands drift to the same side, the receiver chooses a clear station around the avatar so they start at least 3.2 units apart. Pickup descends locally to the stone with a small vertical bow; it no longer arcs back across the avatar's path.

Under **Inspect → Appearance → Traveller → Idle hands**, compare **Finger walks & catch**, **Finger walks only**, **Catch only**, or **Resting hands**. The adjacent **Walking style** selector offers **Both walks**, **Quick spider walk**, and **Awkward upright walk**. Both walks is the default and chooses either style without assigning permanent hand personalities. The rig review offers separate spider and upright modes using the same controller and finger solve with a visible floor.

## Manual previews

**Inspect → Appearance → Traveller → Test idle animations** offers wandering, catch, spider walking, upright walking, and either walk with a stumble. Choose an animation and press **Play animation** to skip the idle timer and distance gate; **Stop preview** ends it. Movement and interaction retain their usual priority, so these controls also exercise disengagement. The normal idle mode and walking-style preference are preserved. Wandering continues until movement or Stop; other previews finish naturally and then observe the shared cooldown.

Catch uses a real available stone and a clear pickup corridor, allowing a testing-only radius of 3.2 units from the hand (ordinary opportunistic pickup remains 1.6). If no stone is available, the menu explains how to try again. Walks need a clear route, and stumble variants force the chance roll while retaining fall clearance checks. Previewing while moving or interacting displays a message instead of stealing the hands.
