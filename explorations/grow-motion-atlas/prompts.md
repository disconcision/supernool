## 01-motion-vocabularies

Use case: stylized-concept. Create a generous landscape research sketch sheet for Grow / nool, a game where symbolic trees become manipulable structures. Beautiful hand-drawn graphite and mineral green/orange colored pencil on warm white paper, precise architectural notebook quality, no photoreal environment. Title "MOTION VOCABULARIES". Five clear horizontal rows of sequential small drawings with arrows, four stages each, generous whitespace. Each row explores ONE structural motion, explicitly not an algebraic axiom. Row labels: "RETRACT / EMERGE", "SLIDE A JOINT", "REATTACH", "EXCHANGE", "WRAP / UNWRAP". Row1: rooted tree with a parent and child fork, children carry small a b c labels, inner fork contracts to parent then emerges on another side; preserve identifiable leaf markers. Row2: a thick curved woody Y-fork whose collar joint slides along the stem while attached limbs follow; overlaid fine dashed skeleton with persistent colored dots. Row3: a branch tip and its two leaves lifts from one socket and is grafted onto another, ghost old attachment, vivid new attachment. Row4: two distinct child branches colored sage and ochre exchange positions through an indicated shallow depth loop; use tiny plan and side-view inset to distinguish crossing from joining. Row5: three small objects on a large parent plate; a smaller containing boundary wraps two of them, then unfolds, then wraps another pair. Show material shells and skeleton separately with pencil transparency. Avoid pretending all rows are equations. No long paragraphs, no extra formulas, only specified short titles and sparse labels. This is an idea sheet comparing local gestures, not a completeness theorem. Visual clarity and substantially different gestures above ornamental detail.

## 02-peeling-and-unweaving

Use case: stylized-concept. Landscape architectural/sculptural concept sketch sheet for Grow / nool. Title "ONE BRANCH, TWO DESCENDANTS". Three horizontal rows, four sequential frames in each, clean ivory paper, graphite construction lines, pale watercolor fills in bark beige, lichen green and muted copper. Rich anatomical drawing but not gore. Row titles exactly "RIBBON PEEL", "BUD & SEPARATE", "UNWEAVE". Each shows a different GEOMETRIC interpretation of one branch being copied, not an algebraic identity. Row1 a thick flat ribbon branch with a simple two-tip fork: seams appear along its thickness, two thinner matching silhouettes peel from one another, settle as two similar forked branches; small cross-section insets make splitting thickness comprehensible. Row2 a woody fork with small round engraved joint; a miniature matching fork buds from a collar, grows to similar size and separates while both stay attached to shared trunk. Row3 a volumetric branch constructed from two entwined wooden strands, both with corresponding fork tips: tight helix, loosening helix, peeling apart, two separate untwisted branches. Show free strand ends, no impossible closed knots. Include colored centerline tracking and ghost previous positions; arrows for temporal order. The result should convey solid volumes and intentional seams rather than branches interpenetrating. Runes are minimal dot/cross patterns, not dense mathematical equations. Frame order obvious. No landscape backdrop. This sheet explores material choreography; matter need not be conserved. High artistic and geometric drawing quality.

Emphasize precise architecture and transition geometry rather than scenic illustration.

Soft cel-shaded silhouettes, painterly detail, tactile sculptural forms, generous whitespace and precise small construction insets.
