Resonant clearing

017 keeps the previous study as a checkpoint and changes completion, feedback, interface and scenery.

A catch you can trust

Pull to 90% along a route to catch it. Overshoot and sideways drift cannot undo a catch. Release to complete; retreat below 55% to uncatch, or Escape to cancel. In body mode, the tree can finish catching up after release. After Space enters hand control, walking beyond the approach circle cannot end the interaction—even between pulls. Escape leaves idle hand control; during a pull it cancels that pull first.

Movement still drives a magical rewrite preview, not physical rope tension. Press Space once to enter hand navigation; arrows choose sigils; hold Space and walk to pull. The entry press does not grip. Release a subsequent grip to return to hand control; Escape exits.

Quiet cues

Quarter-point plucks build toward a catch chime. Appearance offers a recorded catch from the original nool sound bank, volume and Off. The expression uses normal reading notation; the tree shows the grouping.

Make room

Every panel has a minus button that collapses it to a white icon. Help starts collapsed and contains optional hints. The top circle hides all UI; press Escape or backslash to restore it, or use backslash to toggle directly.

A clearing at a fork

Operators use carved medallions; values use pale seed shapes. Mossy slate, small fungi, and three glowing routes frame the tree. Solving it brightens the outgoing routes and lowers their blocking rocks. They suggest further world traversal; no next level is implemented.

The character is unnamed. “Lehi” was an earlier transcript interpretation carried into the prototype.

Beyond the clearing

Appearance now offers a painted elevated view of hills and cliffs, with no horizon or visible sky, a softer version and the original plain backdrop. The other research task’s Icelandic ground reference informed its mossy slate. The earlier eye-level sky composition was rejected for this camera. This is a fixed matte painting, not surrounding playable terrain or a 360-degree skybox.
